How to Optimize Your LinkedIn Profile to Attract Recruiters

Published February 2026  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  8 min read

When a recruiter has a job to fill, one of the first things they do is search LinkedIn. For remote roles this matters even more, because the company cannot meet you in person and your profile is often the first impression you make. A clear, complete profile keeps working for you while you sleep, and an empty or vague one quietly costs you opportunities you never hear about.

The good news is that you do not need to be a "personal branding expert" to fix it. Most profiles improve a lot with a few careful hours. Below is a practical, section-by-section guide to making your LinkedIn profile easier to find and easier to say yes to, written with remote and international job seekers in mind.

1. Use a Clear, Professional Profile Photo

A profile with a photo generally looks more trustworthy than one without, and recruiters are more likely to click on a face than a blank silhouette. You do not need a studio shoot. A clear, well-lit photo of your face and shoulders against a simple background works well. Smile naturally, dress the way people in your field dress, and avoid selfies at arm's length, group photos, heavy filters or sunglasses. If you work in a creative field you can show a bit more personality, but the photo should still be clear.

2. Write a Headline That Says What You Do

Your headline appears next to your name in search results, comments and messages, so it does a lot of work. By default LinkedIn fills it with your current job title, which wastes the space. Use it to explain what you do and for whom, and include the words recruiters are likely to search for.

  • Weak: "Software Engineer at XYZ Company"
  • Stronger: "Full Stack Developer | React and Node.js | Building fast, reliable web apps"
  • Weak: "Looking for opportunities"
  • Stronger: "Customer Support Specialist | Email and Live Chat | Zendesk | Open to remote roles"

Tip: Look at ten job listings for the role you want and note the titles and skills they repeat. Use those exact words in your headline. Recruiters search with those terms, so your profile needs to contain them.

3. Write an About Section People Will Actually Read

Your About section is your short personal pitch. Many people leave it empty or paste in a dull paragraph, so even a clear, honest one puts you ahead. A simple structure works well:

  • First lines: Who you are and what you do. Only the first couple of lines show before the "see more" link, so make them count.
  • Middle: Your strongest skills, the kinds of problems you solve and one or two concrete results.
  • Ending: What you are looking for and how to reach you, for example "I am open to remote customer support roles. Feel free to message me."

Write in the first person, keep it conversational and aim for roughly 150 to 300 words. Include the keywords for your field naturally, without cramming them in.

4. Treat the Experience Section Like Your Resume

Under each role, describe what you achieved, not only what you were responsible for. The same pattern that works on a resume works here: action, what you worked on, and the result. For example, "Handled customer emails" becomes "Answered around 60 customer emails a day and kept first response time under two hours". If you need help phrasing these, our guide on how to write a resume shows the pattern step by step.

If you have little work experience, include projects, volunteering, freelance tasks and relevant courses. LinkedIn lets you add these in their own sections, and they count as real evidence of what you can do.

5. Add the Right Skills and Pin the Best Ones

LinkedIn lets you list a large number of skills, but quality matters more than quantity. Add the tools and abilities that actually appear in the jobs you want, and pin your top three at the start of the list, because those are the ones visitors see first. Skills that colleagues or managers have endorsed look more credible, so it is fine to politely ask people you have worked with to endorse your main ones.

6. Set Up "Open to Work" Properly

LinkedIn lets you tell recruiters you are looking, and you can choose who sees it. The "recruiters only" option shows your status to people using LinkedIn's recruiting tools, while the public option adds a visible badge to your photo. Fill in the details: the job titles you want, the locations, and whether you are open to remote work. This helps you appear in the right searches. If you are currently employed and want to be discreet, note that the recruiter-only setting limits visibility but cannot guarantee that nobody connected to your employer will see it.

7. Collect a Few Genuine Recommendations

A short, specific recommendation from a manager, colleague or client carries more weight than a long list of skills. Ask two or three people who know your work well, and make it easy for them by reminding them of a project and what you would like them to mention. A message like this works well:

  • "Hi Sara, I am updating my LinkedIn profile and would value a short recommendation about the website project we worked on together last year. If it helps, I can send a few points to jog your memory. Thank you either way."

Offer to write one for them too, but only if you mean it.

8. Be Visible Without Being Exhausting

You do not have to become a full-time content creator. A few small habits are enough to show that your profile is alive:

  • Comment thoughtfully on posts from people in your field, adding a real point rather than just "great post".
  • Share one useful thing you learned, a short project update, or an article with your own take on it, once or twice a week if you can.
  • Follow companies you would like to work for, so their openings and news appear in your feed.

Consistency matters more than volume. Recruiters and hiring managers often check recent activity to see whether a person is engaged in their field.

9. Fill In the Small Details

A few quick edits make your profile look finished: set a custom profile URL that contains your name, add your location and, if you apply across time zones, mention your working hours in the About section. Use the Featured section to link to your best work, such as a portfolio, a writing sample or a project page. Turn on contact details so recruiters know how to reach you.

10. Connect and Message With Purpose

When you want to connect with a recruiter or a person at a company you like, add a short personal note instead of sending a blank request. Say who you are, why you are reaching out and what you are asking. For example: "Hello Ahmed, I noticed your team is hiring a content writer. I have written for e-commerce brands for two years and would love to share a few samples if that is helpful." Keep it brief, polite and specific, and do not pressure anyone for a reply.

Remote Job Seekers: Extra Things to Include

  • State clearly that you are open to remote work and list the time zones you can cover.
  • Mention the tools used by remote teams that you know, such as Slack, Zoom, Notion or Google Workspace.
  • List the languages you work in and your level in each.
  • Show evidence of working independently, such as a freelance project or a self-directed course.

Watch Out for Fake Recruiters

As LinkedIn has become the place to find jobs, scammers use it too. Be careful if someone you do not know offers a job without an interview, asks you to move the conversation to WhatsApp or Telegram quickly, or requests money, identity documents or bank details early on. Check that the recruiter's profile looks real, and confirm the job on the company's own website. Our guide on how to avoid remote job scams explains the common patterns.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the headline as a job title only.
  • Using a blurry photo, or none at all.
  • Copying your resume into the About section word for word.
  • Listing skills you cannot back up in an interview.
  • Sending connection requests with no message and then asking for a job.
  • Letting the profile go quiet for months while you are actively applying.

Quick Checklist

  • Clear photo and a headline with your target keywords
  • An About section that tells your story in a few paragraphs
  • Experience entries that show results
  • Relevant skills, with the top three pinned
  • "Open to Work" set up with your preferred roles and remote preference
  • Two or three genuine recommendations
