How to Receive International Payments in Pakistan (2026 Guide)

Published August 2026  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  8 min read

Landing a remote job or a freelance client abroad is only half the battle. Actually getting paid in Pakistan, without losing a large part of your income to fees or waiting weeks for money to arrive, is the other half. Many people only think about this after the first client says "send me your payment details", and by then they are rushing through account setup with a deadline.

This guide explains the main ways to receive international payments in Pakistan in 2026, what each one really costs, and how to set things up before you need them. A caution first: payment services change their features, fees and country support often, so treat the details below as a starting point and confirm current terms on each provider's official site before you rely on them.

What to Compare Before You Choose

The headline fee is rarely the full story. When you compare options, look at four things:

  • Exchange rate. Providers convert dollars or pounds to rupees at their own rate. A rate that is slightly worse than the market rate is a hidden fee.
  • Withdrawal fees. Moving money from your account to your Pakistani bank can have its own charge.
  • Speed. Some methods take a day or two, others a week, and first-time transfers are often slower.
  • Acceptance. The best method on paper is useless if your client cannot pay you through it.

1. Payoneer

Payoneer is one of the most widely used options among Pakistani freelancers because many platforms and remote employers support it directly. You receive local-style receiving details in currencies such as US dollars, and the money can be withdrawn to your Pakistani bank account. It also works well when a client or agency has a payment system built around it.

Withdrawals to a Pakistani bank account typically take a few business days, and the conversion rate is often better than what a local bank gives on a wire. Always check the current rate and any fee on the withdrawal screen before you confirm.

2. Wise

Wise is known for showing the real mid-market exchange rate and charging a clear, upfront fee. It works well for many international transfers, but how much it supports Pakistan for receiving money has limits compared with some other countries. Check what is currently available for a Pakistani resident before you count on it as your main method, and treat it as a useful backup rather than your only option.

3. Payouts Through Freelance Platforms

If you work through a platform such as Upwork or Fiverr, the platform handles the payment from the client and then pays you out. The withdrawal options available to people in Pakistan, such as a direct bank withdrawal or a Payoneer account, differ between platforms and change over time. Open the withdrawal settings in your account and see what is offered, and note the fees, before you take your first job.

4. Direct Bank Wire (SWIFT)

Some employers, especially larger companies, pay by SWIFT wire directly to your Pakistani bank account. It works, but it often involves fees at both the sending bank and the receiving bank, and the exchange rate your local bank applies is usually less favourable than a specialist provider's. If an employer insists on a wire, ask your bank beforehand what they charge for incoming foreign payments and what documents they need.

Understand the Real Cost With a Simple Example

Suppose a client sends you 1,000 US dollars. If the provider's exchange rate is 2% worse than the market rate, you lose about 20 dollars to the rate alone, before any withdrawal fee. That is an invented example to show the arithmetic, but it explains why comparing the rate matters as much as comparing the fee, especially on larger payments.

Set Everything Up Correctly From Day One

  • Use your legal name exactly as it appears on your CNIC when you create accounts, so verification does not fail.
  • Keep your bank account details and IBAN ready before you sign up.
  • Verify your account fully before your first big payment. Unfinished verification is a common cause of delays.
  • Keep a second method available, so an account under review does not block all your income.

Tax and Compliance

Income from remote work and freelancing is still income. Check the current guidance from the Federal Board of Revenue for freelancers, and find out whether registering with the Pakistan Software Export Board or a similar body is relevant to your kind of work, since rules and benefits do change. If your income is growing, an hour with a qualified accountant is usually worth the cost. This guide is general information and not tax or legal advice.

Invoicing Basics for Freelancers

A clear invoice makes you look professional and gets you paid faster. Include your name or business name, the client's name, an invoice number, the date, a short description of the work, the amount and currency, and your payment details. For new clients, consider asking for part of the payment upfront, or splitting a larger project into milestones, so you are not carrying all the risk.

Protect Yourself From Payment Scams

  • Never share your Payoneer, Wise or bank login with anyone, including "middlemen" who offer to help you get paid faster.
  • Do not let someone use your account to move their money. This is a common way people are drawn into fraud, and you can be held responsible.
  • Watch for overpayment tricks. If a client sends more than the agreed amount and asks you to refund the difference, stop. The original payment may later be reversed.
  • Check payment emails carefully. Fake "payment received" messages exist. Confirm the money in your actual account instead of trusting an email.

Local Banks and Foreign Currency Accounts

Whichever service you use, the money eventually lands in a Pakistani bank, so it helps to know how your bank handles incoming foreign payments. Ask your bank which documents it needs for incoming remittances, whether it charges a fee for receiving them, and whether it offers a foreign currency account. Some freelancers keep part of their income in a foreign currency account to avoid converting every payment immediately, but the rules, charges and benefits vary between banks, so ask for the details in writing before you decide.

Which Setup Fits Your Situation?

  • You work through a freelance platform. Start with the platform's own withdrawal options, and add Payoneer if you also want to receive money from clients outside the platform.
  • You work for one overseas employer. Ask how they pay contractors. Many use Payoneer, Wise or a payroll service, and some use bank wires. Choose the option that costs you the least in total.
  • You have several direct clients. Keep two methods open, send clear invoices and track what each client has paid and when.

What to Do If a Payment Is Delayed

Delays happen, especially with a first payment. Do not panic and do not send the same invoice again. First check the status in your account and ask the client for the transfer reference. Then contact the provider's support with the details. Keep screenshots and emails in one folder, since they make any dispute easier to settle. If a client stops responding after the work is delivered, be polite and persistent in writing, and use the platform's dispute process if you worked through one.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Relying on a single payment method. If one account is flagged for review, a backup keeps your income flowing.
  • Ignoring exchange rate differences between providers.
  • Waiting until a client asks before opening accounts.
  • Mixing personal and business money without keeping any records.
