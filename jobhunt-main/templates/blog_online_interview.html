How to Prepare for Online Interviews in 2026

Published May 2026  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  8 min read

Online interviews have become standard for remote hiring, and they come with their own challenges. A shaky connection, poor lighting or a cluttered background can undermine an otherwise excellent candidate. At the same time, a well-prepared online interview can work in your favour, because you are in your own space, with your notes nearby, in an environment you control.

Here is everything you need to prepare for your next online interview, including what to do when technology fails and how to handle the formats that are unique to remote hiring.

1. Test Your Tech at Least 24 Hours Before

Nothing derails an online interview faster than technical problems. Test your camera, microphone and internet connection well before the interview. Join a test call on Zoom, Google Meet or whichever platform will be used, and make sure your audio is clear and your video is stable.

  • Use a wired internet connection if possible, since Wi-Fi can drop at the wrong moment.
  • Close unnecessary tabs and apps to free up bandwidth.
  • Have a backup plan. Keep the interviewer's email or phone number in case of technical failure, and be ready to switch to your phone's mobile data if your internet fails.
  • Charge your laptop fully or keep it plugged in. If power cuts are common where you live, plan for them, for example with a backup for your router.

2. Set Up Your Background and Lighting

Your background says a lot about you. Choose a clean, neutral background, such as a plain wall, a tidy bookshelf or a simple virtual background. Avoid busy, distracting or messy spaces.

Lighting is equally important. Sit facing a window or a light source so your face is well lit. Avoid sitting with a bright light or window behind you, because that turns you into a silhouette, which is surprisingly common in online interviews.

3. Dress Professionally, Fully

Dress as you would for an in-person interview, including the bottom half. You never know when you might need to stand up. Dressing fully also puts you in the right mindset and helps you perform at your best.

Pre-Interview Checklist

  • Camera and microphone tested and working
  • Stable internet connection confirmed
  • Clean, professional background set up
  • Good lighting in front of you
  • Fully dressed professionally
  • Notes and CV open or printed
  • Glass of water nearby
  • Phone on silent
  • Pets and family members informed
  • Time zone and start time double-checked
  • Logged into the platform 5 minutes early

4. Prepare Your Answers in Advance

Research the company thoroughly before the interview. Understand what they do, who their customers are and what their values are. Prepare answers to common questions using the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Have two or three specific examples ready from your experience that show your key skills, write them down and practise saying them out loud. Our guide to the top 10 interview questions gives sample approaches for each one.

5. Make Eye Contact With the Camera

This is one of the most overlooked tips for online interviews. Looking at the interviewer's face on your screen feels natural, but it makes you look as if you are looking downward. Look at the camera lens when you speak, and glance at the screen when you listen. This creates the impression of eye contact.

Tip: Place a small sticker or arrow next to your camera as a reminder to look there when you are talking.

6. Prepare Questions to Ask

Always have three or four questions ready to ask at the end. It shows genuine interest in the role and the company. Good examples: What does success look like in this role after 90 days? How does the team communicate and collaborate remotely? What are the biggest challenges someone in this position typically faces?

Body Language and Voice on Camera

On video, small signals carry a lot of weight. Sit upright, with the camera at eye level, so you do not look down at the lens. Smile when you greet people and nod gently while they speak, since you cannot rely on the subtle cues of an in-person meeting. Speak a little slower than usual, pause after questions and keep your hands calm. If audio delay makes you unsure whether the other person has finished, wait a second before you answer.

A Practice Session That Works

The day before, do a full practice run. Join a call with a friend on the same platform, wear what you plan to wear and answer three or four likely questions. Then record yourself, watch it back and note two things to fix: perhaps your lighting, perhaps a habit like saying "um" too often. One honest practice session usually improves your confidence more than another hour of reading advice.

7. Confirm the Time Zone and the Link

With remote employers, mixed-up time zones are one of the most common causes of missed interviews. When you receive an invitation, check the time zone it uses, add it to your calendar so it converts automatically to your local time, and reply to confirm. Keep the meeting link and any password handy, and open it a few minutes early to make sure it works on your device.

8. Know the Formats You May Meet

  • Live video interview: the standard format, usually with a recruiter or hiring manager.
  • One-way recorded interview: you record answers to set questions with no one on the other end. Practise speaking to the camera, keep answers to about two minutes and use the practice question if one is offered.
  • Screen-sharing task: you complete a task live while sharing your screen. Close personal tabs and notifications first.
  • Take-home task: a short assignment you do on your own. Ask how long it should take, and be wary of a company that asks for a large amount of free work on their real project.

9. What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

Technical problems happen to everyone, and how you handle them shows professionalism. If your connection drops, rejoin quickly and apologise briefly. If it does not come back, send a short message by email or text with your situation and offer to reschedule or switch to a phone call. Stay calm, since interviewers rarely hold a genuine technical problem against a candidate.

10. Avoid Common Mistakes

  • Joining at the last minute and sitting in a rush.
  • Reading answers word for word from your screen. Keep notes as prompts, not a script.
  • Multitasking, checking messages or looking away often.
  • Interrupting because of audio delay. Wait a moment after the other person stops.
  • Forgetting to send a thank-you email afterwards.

After the Interview

Send a short thank-you email within a day. Thank the interviewer, mention one thing you enjoyed discussing and say you are still interested. If they gave you a date for a decision, wait until it passes, then follow up politely. You can also read our tips for landing your first remote job, which cover how to stand out in later stages.
