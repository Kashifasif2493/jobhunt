How to Make a Professional CV in 2026

Published May 2026  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  8 min read

Your CV is usually the first thing a recruiter sees, and in most cases it gets a quick first look before anyone decides whether to read further. That means it has one main job: to make a clear, strong first impression quickly, so the reader wants to know more.

Here is a complete guide to building a professional CV in 2026, with a full example layout, notes on how CV conventions differ between countries, and the common mistakes that cost people interviews. If you are comparing it with a resume, our guide on how to write a resume covers the closely related content side, and this guide focuses on the format and structure of the CV itself.

CV or Resume: What Is the Difference?

In the United States and for most international remote jobs, a "resume" is a one or two page summary and a "CV" is a longer, more detailed record used mainly in academic, medical and research work. In the UK, Pakistan and many other countries, "CV" simply means the document you send to apply for any job. If a listing asks for a CV and says nothing more, send a clear one or two page document. If it asks for a specific format, follow it exactly.

1. Start With the Right Format

In 2026, the most effective CV format is clean, single-column and easy for software to read. Many companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS), which scans CVs before a human reads them. Fancy tables, graphics and multiple columns can confuse some systems, so the safest approach is a simple layout with clear headings, standard fonts such as Arial or Calibri, and plenty of white space.

2. Write a Strong Personal Statement

Your personal statement sits at the top of the CV and is your chance to pitch yourself in three or four sentences. It should answer three questions: Who are you professionally? What are your strongest skills? What are you looking for?

Example: "Digital marketer with three years of experience managing social media campaigns for e-commerce brands. Skilled in content creation, paid ads and analytics. Seeking a remote marketing role with a growth-focused team."

3. List Your Work Experience Correctly

For each role, include the company name, your job title, the dates you worked there and three to five bullet points describing your achievements, not just your duties. Start each bullet with an action verb and include numbers wherever you honestly have them.

  • Weak: "Managed the company's Instagram account"
  • Stronger: "Grew the Instagram following from 2,000 to 18,000 in eight months and increased website visits from social media"

Use real figures only. If you do not have exact numbers, describe the result in plain words rather than inventing a statistic.

4. Education Section

List your highest qualification first, with the institution, degree and year of completion. If you have relevant certifications, such as Google Analytics, HubSpot or a cloud provider's certificate, list them here too. Online certifications can be as useful as formal degrees in many fields, especially when you back them up with a project.

5. Skills Section

Create a dedicated skills section with both hard skills (specific tools and technologies) and a few soft skills that you can support with an example elsewhere on the CV. Match these to the job description as closely as you honestly can. Many recruiters and ATS tools search for specific keywords, so use the exact terms from the posting where they apply to you.

6. Keep the Length Under Control

One page is ideal for early-career applicants, and two pages are fine for longer careers. Every line should earn its place. Remove outdated jobs, irrelevant hobbies and anything that does not support your application for this specific role.

A Complete CV Layout You Can Copy

Here is the order that works for most people:

  • Header: name, email, phone, city and country, LinkedIn or portfolio link
  • Personal statement: three or four sentences
  • Experience: newest first, each with company, title, dates and achievement bullets
  • Projects (optional): especially useful if you have little work experience
  • Skills: tools and abilities, grouped clearly
  • Education and certifications
  • Languages (optional): with your level in each

What a Finished Entry and Skills Block Look Like

Here is an invented example so you can see how the details fit together on the page.

  • Social Media Executive, Bright Retail (Remote), January 2024 to Present
  • Plan and schedule daily content across three brand accounts, using a monthly content calendar
  • Wrote captions and short video scripts that raised average post engagement over six months
  • Report weekly results to the marketing manager and suggest changes based on the numbers
  • Skills: Canva, Meta Business Suite, Google Analytics, content planning, copywriting

Notice that each bullet starts with a verb, describes a result or a clear responsibility and avoids vague words like "responsible for".

Test Your CV Like a Machine Would

Before you send your CV, save it as a PDF, open it, then select all the text and paste it into a plain text file. If the text appears in a sensible order with headings and bullets intact, a basic ATS will probably read it well. If it comes out jumbled, with columns merged or words missing, simplify the layout. This takes two minutes and avoids a common cause of silent rejections.

CV Conventions Differ by Country

If you are applying to employers abroad, adjust to local expectations. In the US and UK, photos, date of birth, marital status, religion and national ID numbers are generally left off a CV, and many employers prefer not to receive them. CVs used in some other countries do include them, but for remote and international applications it is usually better to leave them out. Spend that space on results, tools and projects instead.

Special Situations

  • Little experience: add projects, volunteer work, freelance tasks and relevant courses, and place education higher on the page.
  • Career change: open with a personal statement that explains your new direction, and highlight transferable skills such as communication, organisation and tools.
  • Employment gaps: a short, honest line such as "Career break to care for family" or "Self-study and freelance projects" is better than leaving a long unexplained gap.

7. Proofread, Then Proofread Again

A single spelling mistake can cost you an interview. Read your CV out loud to catch errors, use a spelling tool, and ask a friend to review it. Check that all dates are correct and all links, such as LinkedIn and your portfolio, actually work.

Tailor Your CV for Each Application

Keep one strong master CV, then spend ten minutes adjusting it for each job: update the personal statement, move the most relevant achievements to the top of each role, and make sure the main keywords from the listing appear if they honestly describe you. Tailored applications are usually more effective than sending one generic CV to many companies. Pair the CV with a short cover letter and keep your LinkedIn profile consistent with it.

Common CV Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a photo or personal details that are not required, especially for international roles
  • Listing references in full. "Available on request" is enough, or leave it out
  • Using an unprofessional email address
  • Making it too long, or too crowded with tiny text
  • Sending the same generic CV to every job
  • Using a fancy template that breaks when a system reads it
